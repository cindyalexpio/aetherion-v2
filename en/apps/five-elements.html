Five Elements Matrix

The Generative and Controlling Cycles

The Archive's East–West desk keeps a small, stubborn board of five elemental forces — wood, fire, earth, metal and water — each one feeding the next and, in the same breath, holding a neighbour in check. The board is never still.

When a phase is pressed, the whole circuit rearranges itself around it: what it generates, what generated it, whom it controls, and who controls it. The Archive calls this arrangement Sheng and Ko — the nurturing and the governing turns of the same wheel.

The matrix is a closed circuit. Choose a phase and the chamber rebuilds the entire diagram around it — every feed, every check, laid out like a constellation pinned to the night.

Sheng and Ko

The Two Turns of the Wheel
Ring = Sheng · Star = Ko

Sheng, the generating cycle, moves along the ring — wood feeds fire, fire leaves its ash to earth, earth presses into metal, metal melts and runs as water, and water rises again into wood. Each phase gives birth to its successor and is itself born from its predecessor.

Ko, the controlling cycle, cuts across the ring as a five-pointed star. Wood roots through earth, earth dams water, water quenches fire, fire forges metal, metal hews wood. The board needs both turns at once; take one away and the wheel would race or seize.

Read the ring for nurturing, the star for governance. The two cycles are not opposites but one single pattern seen from two windows.

Field Notes

Guide to the Five Elements Matrix
What this was built for: a neat turning-board for anyone who loves the old maps of correspondence — the idea that wood, fire, earth, metal and water form one closed wheel, each phase nurturing its successor and checking a third. It is presented here as an interactive-fiction artifact of the Archive's "Eastern harmonics" studies; the correspondences are historical and cultural, the effects purely imaginary.
Understanding the cycles
CycleWhat it does
Sheng · GeneratingThe nurturing ring: Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water→Wood. Each phase feeds the next in turn, like a river that never leaves its bed.
Ko · ControllingThe governing star: Wood checks Earth, Earth checks Water, Water checks Fire, Fire checks Metal, Metal checks Wood. Each phase holds a neighbour in balance so no single force runs unchecked.
