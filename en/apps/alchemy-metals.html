Planetary Metals Table

The Seven Metals of the Ancients

Seven metals, seven wandering stars, seven days of the week — the alchemists of the old Archive read the whole cosmos as one ledger. Lead bowed to Saturn, gold answered the Sun, and between them stretched a ladder of correspondences reaching from the forge-floor to the fixed stars.

The chamber keeps a copy of that ledger. Each metal keeps a planet, a day, a guardian, a region of the body and a stage upon the Work. Unfold one and the correspondence is laid out — as though the sky itself had signed its worth in ore.

Pick a metal and the chamber unfolds its planetary dossier — today's weekday is only one of seven turns of the old wheel.

Celestial Correspondence

As Above, So Below
The Metals Below — Earth, heavy, patient
The Seven Stars Above — their wandering lords
[ As above, so below ]

Every metal below wears the seal of a star above. The glow that the sky keeps for a planet, the ore keeps for its master — the same signature twice-written, once in light and once in metal. Time itself is bound into the pattern: each star claims a day, and each day claims a metal, so the whole week is a single slow turn of the Work.

The correspondence is the spine of the archive's alchemy — nothing in the old ledger stands alone, and nothing in the sky is without its earthly echo.

Field Notes

Guide to the Planetary Ledger
What this was built for: a tidy way to hold the classical alchemical correspondence of the seven planetary metals — the idea that each earthy metal answers to one wandering star, one day of the week, one guardian and one region of the body. The table reproduces the archive's copy of an Hermetic ledger, one row per metal, as interactive fiction you can poke at with a thumb.
The sevenfold correspondence
ColumnHow to read it
MetalThe earthly body of the correspondence — Lead to Gold, the ladder the alchemists climbed one rung at a time.
Planet / DayEach metal answers to a wandering star and claims that star's weekday: Saturn's Saturday, Luna's Monday, Sol's Sunday, and the rest of the week's one slow turn.
BodyThe part of the human frame the alchemists bound to each metal — bones and spleen, liver, muscles and blood, kidneys, lungs and brain, and at last the heart.
AttributeA single worthiness-word each metal carries, the moral thread of its correspondence.
GuardianThe classical figure that wields the metal's star — Kronos, Zeus, Ares, Aphrodite, Hermes, Diana, Apollo.
Work StageThe rung of the Great Work each metal holds as its appointed hour, from Calcination to Tincture.
The stage terminology
TagMeaning in this table
First WorkThe opening of the Great Work, when the substance is still being broken down — below the quarter mark.
Middle WorkThe furnace of the middle passage, between the first quarter and the half.
Second WorkThe further refinement, past the halfway point of the operation.
Elixir nearThe threshold of completion — three quarters across, the operation stands on the lip of the Elixir.

Every correspondence is fiction. The planets do not forge our pocket-metal, and no weekday transmutes a single atom.
