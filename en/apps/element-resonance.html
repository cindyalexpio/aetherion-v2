Field Notes

Developer's Guide to This Simulator
What this was built for: a neat trick for anyone who loves chemistry trivia — the idea that you could "tune" any substance the way you tune an instrument. It covers elements, minerals and the vitamins in your medicine cabinet; every sample is a real molecule with a real molar mass. The frequency is a fictional but fully determined function of mass, folded down into the audible band the moment you press the button.
Understanding the controls
ControlWhat it does
Insertion WindowThe search box where you drop a sample. Type part of a name or symbol, pick the match (or press Enter for the top hit); the molar mass is read from the catalogue.
Manual Molar MassAn override in g/mol for anything not listed — type any real molecular weight and it is treated exactly like a dropped sample.
Sweep · Analyze AllThe big button. It computes every sample in the catalogue at once and writes the full ladder of tones — no selection needed. Your device will beep the results are purely numeric.
Reading the results
ResultHow to read it
True ResonanceThe raw note in megahertz from f = K ÷ √M with K = 1.380649 × 10⁹. Far above hearing — matter's own register.
Audible ResonanceThe true note folded down by whole octaves until it lies between 20 Hz and 20 kHz — the note the ear would finally hear.
Musical NoteThe nearest piano pitch to that audible tone.
Octaves DownHow many halvings the chamber performed to reach the audible band.
WavelengthDistance spanned by one full wave of the true resonance — speed of light divided by frequency (λ = c ÷ f).
Spectral ClassA tier given to the mass: Ultralight, Light, Standard, Heavy or Ultraheavy.

Every number on this screen is fiction. The Mössbauer–Karmic constant is a sandbox invention, though the molar masses it consumes are very real.
