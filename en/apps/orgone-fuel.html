Orgone Fuel Simulator

Alternative Thermodynamics Archive

In the mid-20th century, alternative energy pioneers discovered that alternating organic and inorganic geometric layers could passively concentrate a subtle background energy field.

When volatile hydrocarbon fluids—like gasoline—are exposed to this highly concentrated field, historical experiments showed a unique stabilization of the fluid's molecular structures.

This digital sandbox allows you to simulate the exact thermodynamic variables required to turn a standard external fuel canister into a high-density geometric accumulator.

Field Notes

Developer's Guide to This Simulator
What this was built for: a what-if economics sandbox around one of the strangest chapters of 20th-century fringe science — the orgone accumulator. It plays a deliberately silly game: if that folklore truly improved fuel, what would the tank look like, and what would it save at the pump?
Understanding the parameters
ParameterWhat it means
Base Octane RatingThe fuel's grade before treatment. Regular pump gas is about 87; premium is 91–93. Every result is compared against this starting value.
Exposure DurationHow many hours the fuel sits inside the accumulator shell. Longer exposure means deeper (fictional) absorption, with diminishing returns.
Layer PairsHow many alternating "sandwich" shells wrap the canister — one organic layer plus one inorganic layer counts as one pair. Think of it as the thickness of the blanket wrapped around the fuel.
Ambient TemperatureHow warm the storage area is. The effect is modelled as peaking near 70 °F and fading further away from it.
Fuel VolumeHow many gallons your canister holds. Changes the dollar figures only, not the fuel chemistry.
Local $/GallonWhat you actually pay at the pump. Feeds the savings maths only.
Reading the results
ResultHow to read it
New OctaneYour starting octane plus the simulated boost — a fictional rise, capped so it never becomes truly absurd.
Efficiency GainThe percentage of extra engine-burn efficiency the simulator credits the treated fuel with.
Projected MPGExtra miles per gallon versus a 25 mpg baseline — the "so what" number in driving terms.
$/Gallon EffectivePump price divided by the efficiency gain: what each gallon effectively costs once it goes further.
Savings / TankCash saved on one fill-up of the volume you entered.
Annual SavingsThat per-tank figure × 52 weekly fill-ups — an optimistic yearly total.

Every number on this screen is fiction. The wording borrows 1950s jargon, but the maths is a made-up sandbox.
