Lakhovsky Multiwave Oscillator

The Broad-Band Radiant Cell Tuner

The Archive's electro-biologists keep a single leaf of notes from the laboratory of Georges Lakhovsky, the engineer who swore that every living cell is a tiny capacitor. His claim was disarmingly simple: the cell membrane stores charge like condenser plates, the fluid inside acts as the dielectric, and each cell rings at its own natural frequency — the way a tuned circuit rings.

Illness, by his reading, is nothing but a loss of oscillation amplitude. The Multiwave Oscillator answers it not with a single note but with an entire radiant band — concentric rings struck with sparks, re-tuning whatever amplitude the cell has lost. The device was ridiculed, then quietly suppressed. The Archive holds its only surviving calibration table.

Adjust the broad-band coil against the surviving table and read the cell's estimated response — a single, fictional calibration between the spark gap and the living tissue.

The Cell as Condenser

Every Living Cell is a Capacitor
Cell Membrane — the Condenser Plates
Intracellular Fluid — the Dielectric
[ Natural resonance = 1 / (2π √LC) ]

Every living cell is a micro-condenser. The membrane is its pair of plates; the intracellular fluid is the dielectric sandwiched between them; and together they form a resonant circuit with a natural frequency of its own. Lakhovsky could state the whole law in a single line: f = 1 / (2π √LC).

Health, then, is a full, lively oscillation; disease a damped one — an oscillation running down like a struck bell left to fade. The therapy of the Multiwave Oscillator was not to apply a chosen frequency but to bathe the tissue in every frequency at once, coaxing the stale cell back to its own song.

The cell is the condenser, the fluid is the dielectric, and the coil's entire job is to re-amplify whatever the cell can no longer ring on its own.

Field Notes

Developer's Guide to the Calibration Room
What this was built for: a neat room for anyone who loves the stranger corners of gadget history — the Multiwave Oscillator of Georges Lakhovsky, the condenser model of the living cell, and the idea that a spark across a set of concentric rings could "re-tune" an ailing tissue. Every number you meet here is determined fiction; the calibration table is the only record of its kind to survive the suppression.
Understanding the controls
ControlWhat it does
Wave Band (λ index)The dial 1–20 that picks which band of the radiant scale the coil is read on. The wavelength shown is always a third of a metre divided by the index — λ = 300 cm ÷ n — a purely fictional mapping.
Radiant IntensitySets how hard the coils are driven, from standby to full glow. The coil drawing pulses with it, and so does the estimated cell response.
Graph the Band SpreadLays out all twenty bands at once — index, wavelength, and the register name the archive assigned to each living tone.
Reading the response
ResultHow to read it
Cell ResponseThe re-amplified reading in percent — directly the intensity you set, presented as if it had been measured at the bench.
Amplitude RestoredA bar of the same value. Above the midpoint the archive marks the tone re-amplified; below it, still damped.
RegisterThe name from the archive's table of living tones for the band you are reading.

Every reading is fiction. Lakhovsky's coils were ridiculed and then forgotten; the Archive pretends, only for a page, that they were never quite rubbed out.
