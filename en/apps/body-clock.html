The Body Clock

The Chinese Organ Clock · A Daily Tuning

The physicians of old did not measure the body by pulses alone. They timed it — as one would time a fixed harmonicon, wound once and left to circle. Every two hours the "governor" passes from one organ to the next: a perfect hand laid across the wrist of the day.

Pause beside the clock and the chamber reveals which meridian rules the hour, which emotion rides its current, and the two points where that current can be felt most plainly under the skin.

The hour is a room. Step inside it, read the organ that governs it, and touch the two points where the day's pulse is loudest.

The Twenty-Four Hour Cycle

Twelve Meridians · Two Hours Each
24hfixed
cycle

The day unwinds as a single closed wheel. Twelve meridians, each allotted two hours, pass the seat of command one to the next like a lamp handed down a corridor. Follow the ring clockwise and the hours follow the body literally from the crown of the lungs to the seat of the liver.

At every moment exactly one governor sits in the seat of power — and exactly two points on the body are said to carry its clearest signal.

Developer's Guide to the Clock
What this was built for: a neat console for anyone who loves the old idea that the body runs on a timetable. The twelve windows below are the traditional two-hour "governors"; the point names are the classical acupressure stations. As with the whole Archive, this adapts the meridian system as fiction for entertainment — a reverent souvenir of a lost medical art, not a manual for one.
