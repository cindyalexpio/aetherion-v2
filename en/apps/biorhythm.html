The Three Cycles of Dr. Fliess

In the late years of the nineteenth century, Dr. Wilhelm Fliess of Berlin proposed that every human life is governed by three hidden clocks — set running at the very first breath. A physical cycle of twenty-three days, an emotional cycle of twenty-eight, an intellectual cycle of thirty-three; three fixed drums beating beneath the skin for as long as we live.

The orthodox guilds of medicine dismissed the notion and, in time, the Archive buried it as one more oddity of fringe science. The chamber here recreates his charts precisely — the same sine traces, the same crossings of the zero line, the days the old theorists once called critical.

Three clocks wind at birth. On the day a curve crosses the midline, the old manuals warned of danger and strain — the so-called critical day.

The three clocks nest inside one another like the chambers of a watch. The physical drum turns fastest — twenty-three days to a revolution; the emotional follows at twenty-eight; the intellectual turns slowest of all, thirty-three days from crest to crest. None of them can be heard, yet the Archive insists they can be traced.

Forged on the day of birth, each clock repeats forever. The recorder plots all three at once, so their crossings can be read at a glance.

Field Notes

Developer's Guide to This Recorder
What this was built for: a small archive tool for anyone who loves the history of the fringes — the notion, born with Dr. Wilhelm Fliess in the 1890s, that three fixed cycles begin on the day of birth, one in the body, one in the emotions, one in the mind. The periods — 23, 28 and 33 days — are historical numbers; the chart drawn here is a fictional but fully determined set of traces, reproduced as archive fiction.
Understanding the three cycles

PhysicalRevolves every 23 days. In the old doctrine it was tied to the body, strength and endurance — here it is simply a sine trace in gold.
EmotionalRevolves every 28 days, the figure once assigned to mood and feeling. Drawn in teal.
IntellectualRevolves every 33 days — the slowest of the three, once said to govern thought and judgement. Drawn in violet.
Origin pointThe birth date you enter. Every cycle starts at the first breath and repeats forever from that moment.
